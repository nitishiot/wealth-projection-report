# Fund due-diligence & keep/replace logic

## How to vet a single fund

Check five things, in order of how much they matter for a long hold:

1. **Manager & tenure.** Who runs it, since when, and is that record theirs? A
   **recent manager departure is a major red flag** — the track record you're
   relying on was built by someone who may be gone. Treat a fund as "a different
   fund than its history" for a year or two after a lead-manager exit. Watch for
   *key-person concentration*: if one manager runs several of the user's funds
   (e.g. the same CIO across two schemes), a single departure hits multiple
   holdings at once.

2. **Style.** Concentrated + momentum (few stocks, high top-5 weight) = high
   alpha potential but violent drawdowns and higher blow-up risk. Diversified
   (60-80 stocks) = steadier. Neither is "better" in the abstract — but a
   concentrated fund is *not* the same risk as a diversified peer, so don't
   compare them as if it were.

3. **AUM vs category capacity.** Size is a structural, permanent drag in
   **small-caps** especially — a very large small-cap fund can't take meaningful
   positions in genuinely small companies and drifts toward mid-caps. Mid and
   large-&-mid tolerate size better; flexicaps best of all. AUM bloat compounds
   against you every year, unlike a manager exit which you can react to later.

4. **Risk-adjusted metrics.** Sharpe, Sortino, max drawdown and recovery, std
   dev vs benchmark. A higher trailing return with a much worse drawdown is not
   an upgrade.

5. **Cost & structure.** Direct plan, expense ratio, style drift vs mandate.

## Red-flag checklist
- Recent lead-manager exit / reshuffle (check across *all* the user's funds).
- AUM bloat relative to category (small-cap red line first).
- Extreme concentration (top-5 > ~40%, < ~25 stocks) in a category where peers diversify.
- Style drift (a "mid-cap" fund quietly holding large-caps, or vice versa).
- A one-year collapse vs benchmark driven by concentrated sector bets.
- Key-person concentration: few managers running many of the user's funds.

## "Same risk, better return" — set expectations
Nearly a free lunch; markets rarely offer it. What you *can* realistically do:
(a) improve **risk-adjusted** return (better Sharpe/drawdown), and (b) fix a
structural flaw (bloat, manager gap, concentration). Frame recommendations that
way. A fund with a higher headline number but higher volatility is a
*different risk*, not a free upgrade — say so.

## Keep / replace / upgrade logic
- **Same risk** ≈ same category + similar volatility. A like-for-like swap that
  barely changes risk should be justified by a real edge (capacity, manager
  stability, lower drawdown), not a slightly higher trailing number.
- **Better portfolio, not just better fund.** Often the biggest win is adding a
  missing category (flexicap core, international exposure, large-cap/index
  ballast) rather than swapping within a category. All-aggressive, overlapping
  line-ups usually lack a stabiliser.
- **Fund the switch tax-efficiently.** Selling triggers **LTCG at 12.5% above
  ₹1.25L/yr** plus any exit load — prefer redirecting *future* contributions to
  the new fund, and stagger multiple switches across tax years.
- **Sequence upgrades by severity.** A manager-exit + concentrated fund that
  already blew up is a clear replace; a fund that merely lost a manager but held
  up (e.g. large-cap-cushioned) is a "watch / replace second", not urgent.

## Structural / category conventions (India, SEBI)
- **Large & Mid Cap**: min 35% large + min 35% mid at all times → forced mid-cap
  floor → falls harder in corrections than a flexicap.
- **Flexicap**: min 65% equity, no cap-wise minimums → typically large-cap heavy,
  can raise cash / hold international → shallower drawdowns; better turmoil
  cushion, tends to lag in strong bull runs. Good defensive core; poor
  substitute for a *dedicated* mid/small-cap slot.
- **Small Cap**: highest return ceiling and deepest drawdowns; capacity-constrained.
- Two funds in one category = overlap; count effective *managers*, not funds.
