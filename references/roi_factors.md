# Factors that drive / predict long-run ROI

Use this to frame expectations honestly. Fund history is only one input. When a
user says "just analyse the fund's history to predict 10-20 year returns," gently
correct: history reveals a fund's *character*, but the rupee outcome is driven at
least as much by valuation at entry/exit, macro, costs, and the investor's own
behaviour. Present the range, not a single number.

## 1. Fund track record (necessary, not sufficient)
- Long-term CAGR across 3/5/7/10-yr and since-inception.
- **Rolling returns** (not point-to-point) — measures consistency, not luck of end dates.
- Performance across full cycles, especially drawdowns (2008, 2020, 2022, 2025-26).
- Downside capture vs the index in bad years.
- Persistence of alpha over the benchmark (e.g. Nifty Smallcap 250 TRI, Midcap 150 TRI).

## 2. Risk-adjusted metrics (history is meaningless without these)
- Standard deviation (volatility), Sharpe, Sortino.
- Beta vs benchmark, maximum drawdown and recovery time.
- Alpha and whether it persists.

## 3. Fund construction & management quality
- Fund manager tenure and record — **a great record under a manager who has left is not your future.**
- AUM size vs category capacity — critical for small/mid caps (bloat = drag).
- Concentration, turnover, cash levels, number of holdings.
- Expense ratio (direct plans compound a big edge over 20 years).
- Style discipline vs drift (SEBI category definitions).

## 4. Category & structural factors
- Small vs mid vs large-&-mid vs flexicap behave very differently in return and drawdown.
- Overlap risk: several funds in the same category = less diversification than the count implies.
- Where the category sits in its valuation cycle.
- Liquidity of underlying stocks.

## 5. Macro & market factors (biggest 10-20 yr swing factor, and NOT in fund history)
- India GDP growth, corporate earnings growth, interest rates, inflation.
- **Valuation at entry and exit** — starting/ending P/E dominates long-run outcomes.
- Currency, FII/DII flows, global cycles, tax policy (LTCG currently 12.5% above ₹1.25L/yr).

## 6. Investor behaviour (often the #1 real-world determinant)
- SIP/annual discipline vs stopping in downturns.
- **Step-up** (raising the annual contribution) vs flat — the single biggest lever the user controls.
- Staying invested through volatility; rebalancing.
- Sequence-of-returns risk near the goal date.

## 7. Statistical method used to project
- Assumed CAGR (conservative/base/optimistic), real vs nominal.
- Lump-sum vs XIRR on staggered contributions; single-point vs Monte Carlo.

## The honest caveat to always state
SEBI-mandated and true: **past performance does not guarantee future returns.**
Build projections on tempered assumptions, show a scenario range, and flag that
macro/valuation and the user's own behaviour will drive the actual outcome more
than any back-tested CAGR.
